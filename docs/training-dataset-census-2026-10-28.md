# Public workload and training-data census — October 28, 2026

## Decision

We found many public benchmark suites, but no external dataset that is ready to train ISAForge's candidate selector with comparable labels. ISAForge's target is **candidate-level, whole-program PicoRV32 speedup plus integrated resource and routed-timing outcomes**, under our compiler, PCPI interface, fixed inputs, correctness checks, and iCE40 flow. Published cycle/PPA data from another core, FPGA family, or RTL flow are useful method/feature references, not interchangeable target labels.

The best immediate path is to qualify genuinely new application sources and create new labels through the existing pipeline. A new source becomes an ISAForge training group only after pinned inputs, license, independent oracle, RV32 compatibility, fixed memory fit, legal candidate extraction, and the public opportunity gate are established before candidate measurements.

## Large ML-for-HLS datasets checked separately

These projects add many measured rows, but their labels describe HLS-generated accelerators or FPGA physical-design configurations, not custom-instruction speedup on the fixed PicoRV32/PCPI system. Do not merge their area, latency, or resource labels into the ISAForge target table.

| Dataset/system | Published or repository scope | ISAForge use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [ForgeHLS](https://github.com/zedong-peng/ForgeHLS), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03255) | Public HLS dataset with over 400k designs from 846 kernels; pragma exploration and HLS QoR targets. | Useful reference for dataset versioning and kernel-level generalization. HLS pragma/QoR labels do not transfer to PicoRV32 instruction speedup or iCE40 core-integrated area. |
| [wa-hls4ml](https://huggingface.co/datasets/fastmachinelearning/wa-hls4ml), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05615) | More than 680k fully connected/convolutional networks synthesized with hls4ml for Xilinx FPGA resource and latency estimation. | Distinct accelerator design space, feature representation, toolchain, FPGA family, and target. Not speedup/hardware-cost labels for this instruction-selection task. |
| [HLSDataset](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10977), [data release](https://github.com/UT-LCA/ML4Accel-Dataset/tree/main/fpga_ml_dataset/HLS_dataset) | C/SystemC HLS sources, reports, and post-route prediction examples for a particular FPGA flow. | The paper itself identifies its one-device scope. It can guide provenance and dataset practices, not cross-platform label pooling. |
| [HLSyn](https://github.com/UCLA-DM/HLSyn) | 42 HLS kernels and more than 42,000 pragma configurations with LUT/FF/DSP/BRAM and performance fields. CC BY-SA 4.0 dataset. | Potential auxiliary resource-model data only; labels are HLS accelerator QoR for specific AMD/Xilinx tools, not PicoRV32 custom-instruction area or whole-program speedup. |
| [OpenABC-D](https://github.com/NYU-MLDA/OpenABC) | Hardware graph and synthesis-recipe corpus with technology-mapped area/delay labels. BSD-3-Clause repository license. | Optional hardware-representation research data only; no application execution or custom-instruction speedup labels. Inspect licenses of individual hardware sources before reuse. |
| [HLSFactory](https://github.com/sharc-lab/HLSFactory) | Framework and source adapters for PolyBench, MachSuite, CHStone and other HLS datasets. | A way to assemble HLS studies, not an independent set of ISAForge-compatible measured outcomes. Its C kernels can be source-review leads only. |

The fact that these datasets are much larger does not make them more relevant to the target. The next valid training rows must be produced by the same compiler/ISA, candidate-to-PCPI lowering, correctness verifier, PicoRV32 cycle measurement, and integrated synthesis/routing path. Transfer-learning from HLS labels would require a separately justified source/target mapping experiment; no such need is demonstrated by the current measurements.

The SNU-RT lineage link above is not a training recommendation: it documents that several SNU-RT-origin kernels are already collected in TACLeBench. Dataset names alone do not establish independent workload evidence.

## Intake contract for new data

For every proposed source, store source URL/commit and license; exact program/input/oracle hashes; compiler, ISA, flags, simulator, core, PCPI interface, RAM and physical-flow identity; candidate formulas/features and family exclusions; baseline and candidate whole-program cycles; independent correctness result; integrated LUT4/FF/RAM/carry; routing seeds and minimum Fmax; charged attempt/failure status; and actual build/sim/synthesis/route wall times. Keep architecture and toolchain as explicit metadata. Never pool raw LUT/Fmax across FPGA families as one scale.

Keep measurement-time training data distinct from source-only discovery data. A passing baseline/discovery is not a candidate outcome label. Keep censored failures unknown, not negative speedups. For model evaluation use entire workload/family holdouts, same visible information and selection budget, feasible best-so-far performance, oracle regret only for complete pools, and actual evaluation cost. Candidate rows, seeds, parameter sets, and contexts do not substitute for independent workloads.

## Shortlist and next actions

1. The October 28 expanded search added LLVM Richards, jsmn, heatshrink, tiny-regex-c, and PULPino Sudoku to the prior MiBench/CHStone/MachSuite census. Tiny-regex-c and Sudoku both completed 12-candidate studies, adding 24 held-out measurements but only two workload/source groups. Queens was rejected by its unchanged pool/opportunity gate. Richards remains the strongest unqualified source-only lead; jsmn and heatshrink remain blocked by stack/memory constraints. Preserve these as source-screen findings, not target-tested rejections.
2. Tiny-regex-c and Sudoku outcomes are now consumed development evidence. Do not rerun them as prospective evaluation targets or tune a model on them and relabel their scores as unseen-workload evidence. They may enter a later training release only under a new source/family-grouped split. Viterbi's standard configuration remains closed by the exact 8-KiB memory mismatch above. Reconsidering its commented smaller dimensions would require a separately declared parameterized context and fresh source-only review; it is not a waiver. GSM needs a different input contract and is not admissible under the current gate.
3. Resume new-source qualification only when source-only review establishes a plausible workload that can pass the existing RV32I/Zicsr ILP32 `-Os`, 8 KiB RAM, 6,400-byte code/data, 1,664-byte stack, legal-pool and opportunity constraints without modifying the workload to fit.
4. For the next fresh workload, freeze the prompt/tool version, zero-shot predictions, memory, constraints, budgets, and matched frequency, merit, latency-cost, random, non-agent active-learning, and agent policies before any target outcome. Measure correctness, whole-program cycles, integrated area, routed timing and actual evaluation time under the same available information and budget. Do not train on synthetic formulae or unrelated published PPA to inflate the sample count.
5. With six eligible independent source families still the confirmatory target, treat fewer new workloads as pilot/development data and keep uncertainty explicit. The current blocker is workload eligibility, not a shortage of benchmark names.

This read-only census itself created no labels. The historical pre-holdout corpus remains at 122 verified labels / 126 charged attempts / 14 workload groups. The subsequent tiny-regex-c and PULPino Sudoku campaigns add 24 measured candidate outcomes on two separate source groups; those outcomes were not used to fit their own frozen selectors and are not 24 independent workload replications. The current ML and agent findings are in the [model/literature update](agent-ml-literature-update-2026-10-28.md) and [Sudoku selection report](../reports/2026-10-28-pulpino-sudoku-selector-r4/README.md).
