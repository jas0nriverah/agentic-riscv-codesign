# Measured ranking, transfer, and uncertainty — October 20, 2026

This focused extension to the [research foundation](research-foundation.md) reads primary method sections and distinguishes adaptations from reproductions. It informs one fixed ranking comparison, not a search for another model version. The [project contract](../README.md) remains authoritative. The exact Sun 2004 and Hussein 2026 full-method access gaps remain unresolved after another search; neither is filled by treating its abstract as methods.

## Primary methods and what we can use

**Chen, Zheng, Yan, Jiang, Moreau, Ceze, Guestrin, and Krishnamurthy. “Learning to Optimize Tensor Programs.” NeurIPS 31, 2018.** Read [arXiv v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.08166v4), §§3–4, Algorithm 1, and §6. The cost model learns measured tensor-program runtime; the GBT implementation uses XGBoost. Equation 2 uses pairwise logistic ranking instead of squared runtime error. Search combines model-guided simulated annealing, diversity selection, and random exploration. Transfer uses program representations shared across workloads, with global and local models. Their bootstrap uncertainty experiments do **not** establish an acquisition advantage. ISAForge can adapt measured-outcome pairwise ranking and separate transfer from local adaptation, using its existing finite pools. Tensor scheduling spaces, GPU measurements, and that paper's transfer results do not establish RISC-V instruction-selection performance. Our fixed ranker is an adaptation of the objective, not an AutoTVM reproduction.

**Hutter, Hoos, and Leyton-Brown. “Sequential Model-Based Optimization for General Algorithm Configuration.” LION 5, 2011, pp. 507–523. DOI: [10.1007/978-3-642-25566-3_40](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-25566-3_40).** Read the authors' [extended version](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~hutter/papers/10-TR-SMAC.pdf), model/search and instance-feature sections. SMAC combines a random-forest surrogate, expected improvement, random challengers, and comparison of configurations across instances. Bootstrap trees provide empirical predictive mean/variance; log-runtime transformation requires care when the endpoint is arithmetic runtime. Its instance features distinguish problem settings. For ISAForge, this strengthens the case for a non-agent model-based search baseline and explicit workload context. Twenty bootstrapped XGBoost regressors are not SMAC, and their spread is not automatically calibrated confidence. Reproducing SMAC's intensification would require repeated-instance observations absent from today's six-candidate pools. Defer another forest implementation until a larger measured pool can distinguish acquisitions.

**Feurer, Letham, and Bakshy. “Scalable Meta-Learning for Bayesian Optimization.” 2018 preprint, [arXiv:1802.02219v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.02219v1).** Read §§3–4.3, especially Equations 1–3. RGPE fits separate source GPs and a target GP, standardizes predictions, and weights models by sampled probability of minimum pairwise ranking loss on **observed target points**. The target model uses leave-one-out prediction; poor source models can be discarded to limit weight dilution. Ensemble variance uses squared weights, and EI drives search. The useful lesson is to measure negative transfer and compare historical-only ranking with paid adaptation. RGPE weighting needs more than one target observation and a shared configuration/feature domain; it is not a zero-shot selector or a justification for reading hidden target labels. Keep as a later transfer baseline after workload diversity improves. The reviewed version is explicitly the nine-page 2018 preprint, not the subsequently expanded arXiv version.

**Mendis, Renda, Amarasinghe, and Carbin. “Ithemal: Accurate, Portable and Fast Basic Block Throughput Estimation using Deep Neural Networks.” ICML 2019, PMLR 97, pp. 4505–4515.** Read [§§3–4](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/mendis19a/mendis19a.pdf). Two hierarchical LSTMs encode instruction tokens and blocks. Labels come from isolated blocks repeated 100 times, with cache/context-switch checks matching analytical baseline assumptions; binary duplicates are removed across applications. Training uses normalized absolute error and a random block split. These are steady-state x86 block-throughput labels, not whole-program custom-instruction speedup or unseen-workload evidence. Adopt the measurement-contract and deduplication discipline. Keep ISAForge's whole-workload exclusions and whole-program processor labels; do not import their random-block split or introduce a neural model for this small dataset.

**Tibshirani, Barber, Candès, and Ramdas. “Conformal Prediction Under Covariate Shift.” NeurIPS 32, 2019.** Read [§§2–3, Corollary 1](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/file/8fb21ee7a2207526da55a679f0332de2-Paper.pdf). Weighted conformal inference reweights residual/nonconformity scores by the test-to-training covariate-density ratio, including test-point mass at infinity. The guarantee assumes unchanged conditional outcome distribution, suitable support, and the specified weights; ordinary conformal prediction assumes exchangeability. ISAForge's workload shifts and tiny dependent candidate pools do not satisfy these conditions merely because we bootstrap trees. First report held-out interval coverage, width, and missed optima as diagnostics. A calibrated claim needs independently held-out workload groups and a justified sampling model. Adding a conformal wrapper now would not repair missing data or establish coverage under arbitrary workload shift.

**Lam, Shoaib, and Srikanthan. “Modeling Arbitrator Delay-Area Dependencies in Customizable Instruction Set Processors.” DELTA 2006.** Read [§§4–5](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2006/01/Modeling-Arbitrator-Delay-Area-Dependencies-in-Customizable-Instruction-Set-Processors.pdf). The Nios II study models arbitration delay as the instruction count changes. Its runtime comparison includes cycles multiplied by the feasible clock period; the area model includes arbitration overhead. Validation uses repeated custom-instruction calls and platform-specific synthesis/simulation. This is a direct precedent for integrated timing/area accounting: isolated datapath cost misses shared-interface overhead. Its fitted constants and Nios II topology cannot be transferred to PicoRV32 PCPI. Reuse the principle when building the latency-aware baseline and jointly measuring sets; individual candidate speedups and LUT deltas cannot simply be added.

## Implementation decision

The demonstrated problem is that the existing selector has not beaten merit, sometimes ties random acquisition, and has no measured ranking comparison. Add **one fixed XGBoost pairwise ranker**, preserving the regression ensemble and feature schema. Group pairs by workload/build/input context; derive relevance order only from measured training speedups; hold out the target workload and exclude related operation-tree families. Rank scores must never be displayed as predicted speedup. The [XGBoost ranking documentation](https://xgboost.readthedocs.io/en/stable/tutorials/learning_to_rank.html) defines `qid`, pairwise objectives, and pair sampling; the installed replay dependency is pinned separately.

Compare frozen zero-shot regression and ranking, paid mean/UCB adaptation, frequency, merit, and random at identical query budgets. Include interval coverage/width as diagnostics, baseline fallback, feasibility checks, historical measurement cost, inference time, and best verified speedup/regret. This is a **consumed-data development pilot** while only two supported workload groups exist. It does not bypass the three-group gate in the primary corpus evaluator or justify broad generalization claims. No hyperparameter sweep, area model, or tuned uncertainty multiplier is introduced.

The stronger baseline roadmap remains Gardner-style constrained EI, PicoRV32 latency/timing-aware merit with charged calibration, then cost-aware acquisition and a bounded LLM selection agent. All-feasible tiny pools cannot establish a constrained-optimization advantage. Additional memory-compatible programs and broader candidate pools are the next data requirement, before model complexity or novelty claims.
